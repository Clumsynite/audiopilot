The right mic and speakers, automatically.

AudioPilot lives in your Mac's menu bar. It switches to the devices you actually want, keeps AirPods out of low-quality call mode, and mutes every microphone with one shortcut.

Devices

  • Learns your priority and switches input and output as devices come and go.
  • Blocks Bluetooth mics so AirPods keep high-quality audio.
  • Rules per app, and profiles that follow your Wi-Fi network.
  • Per-app output and volume (macOS 14.2+).

Mute

  • ⌘⇧M mutes every mic: toggle or push-to-talk.
  • Globe key, a mouse button, an AirPods stem, or automatically when a call starts.
  • See which app is using the mic; Zoom and Teams mute sync.

Everywhere

  • ⌘K command palette and global device shortcuts.
  • Spotlight and Shortcuts actions, plus audiopilot:// links.
  • Updates itself, signed and verified, when you're not on a call.

Install

AudioPilot isn't from the App Store, so macOS asks you to confirm it once. After that it updates itself.

  1. Download and open the disk imageDrag AudioPilot to Applications, then open it from there. A mic icon appears in the menu bar.
  2. If macOS says it can't verify the appClick Done, open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to AudioPilot.
  3. Turn on what you needAudioPilot only asks for a permission (Microphone, Accessibility, System Audio Recording…) when you switch on a feature that needs it.

Questions

How do updates work?

AudioPilot checks this site every few hours. A new version is downloaded, checked against its published signature and digest, and installed when no app is using the mic, or when you quit. You can turn this off in Settings › General › Updates.

Why does macOS show a recording indicator?

Only per-app audio captures sound, to play an app through another device or at another volume. It's off by default; while it's off, AudioPilot records nothing.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit AudioPilot and move it to the Bin. Its settings are in ~/Library/Application Support/AudioPilot.
